# Monetary Policy Framework in India

Under the Monetary Policy Framework Agreement signed in 2015 and codified via amendments to the **RBI Act, 1934** in 2016, India transitioned to a formal **Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT)** regime.

## Statutory Mandate and Target
- **Primary Objective**: Maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth (Section 45ZA).
- **Inflation Target**: **4% headline CPI inflation** with a tolerance band of **+/- 2%** (i.e., **2% to 6%**).
- **Failure Condition**: Inflation remaining above 6% or below 2% for **three consecutive quarters**. The RBI must report to the Central Government explaining causes, remedial actions, and estimated time to target attainment.

## Composition of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)
Under Section 45ZB of the RBI Act:
- **6 Members**:
  - Governor of RBI (Ex-officio Chairperson).
  - Deputy Governor in charge of monetary policy.
  - One officer of RBI nominated by the Central Board.
  - Three external members appointed by Central Government for a 4-year term (not eligible for reappointment).
- **Decision Rule**: Majority voting. Each member has one vote; the Governor has a **casting vote** in case of a tie.
- **Transparency**: Minutes containing individual voting records and statements published on the 14th day after every meeting.

## Key Monetary Policy Instruments

### 1. Policy Rates & Liquidity Adjustment Facility (LAF)
- **Policy Repo Rate**: Interest rate at which RBI lends short-term funds to scheduled commercial banks against eligible government securities.
- **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)**: Introduced in 2022 to absorb excess liquidity without collateral requirement. Sits **25 bps below** the Repo rate (replaces fixed-rate reverse repo as floor of the LAF corridor).
- **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)**: Overnight emergency borrowing window for banks dipping into SLR quota. Sits **25 bps above** the Repo rate (ceiling of LAF corridor).
- **LAF Corridor**: MSF (Ceiling) ↔ Repo Rate (Center) ↔ SDF (Floor), spanning 50 bps.

### 2. Reserve Ratios
- **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)**: Share of Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) banks must park as cash with RBI. No interest is paid by RBI on CRR.
- **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)**: Percentage of NDTL that banks must maintain in liquid assets (cash, gold, approved government securities).

### 3. Open Market Operations (OMOs)
- Buying and selling of Government securities (G-Secs) by RBI to inject or absorb permanent liquidity.
- **Operation Twist**: Simultaneous purchase of long-term G-Secs and sale of short-term G-Secs to flatten the sovereign yield curve.

## Channels of Monetary Transmission
1. **Interest Rate Channel**: Changes in repo rate pass on to bank lending and deposit rates (accelerated by the External Benchmark Lending Rate - EBLR mechanism since 2019).
2. **Credit Channel**: Influencing volume of credit extended to businesses and households.
3. **Asset Price Channel**: Impact on equity and real estate valuations affecting wealth sentiment.
4. **Exchange Rate Channel**: Differential interest rates affecting capital flows and currency valuation.
