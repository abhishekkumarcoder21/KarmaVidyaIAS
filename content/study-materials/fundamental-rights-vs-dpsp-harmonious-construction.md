# Fundamental Rights vs Directive Principles: The Doctrine of Harmonious Construction

The relationship between **Part III (Fundamental Rights)** and **Part IV (Directive Principles of State Policy)** forms the core of constitutional philosophy in India. While Fundamental Rights guarantee civil liberties and political democracy, Directive Principles mandate socio-economic democracy and the creation of an egalitarian welfare state.

## Constitutional Distinction

| Dimension | Fundamental Rights (Part III) | Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Nature** | Negative injunctions (restrain State action) | Positive obligations (instruct State action) |
| **Justiciability** | Enforceable in courts (Art. 32 & Art. 226) | Non-justiciable in courts (Art. 37) |
| **Objective** | Political democracy and personal liberty | Social and economic democracy |
| **Suspension** | Suspended during Emergency (except Arts. 20 & 21) | Cannot be suspended; enduring state goals |

## The Judicial Evolution: From Subordination to Harmonious Synthesis

The legal debate centered on whether an enforceable Fundamental Right must yield to an unenforced Directive Principle when the two appear in conflict.

### Phase 1: Subordination of DPSPs
- **State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan (1951)**: The Supreme Court held that Fundamental Rights are sacrosanct and supreme; the Directive Principles must conform to and remain subsidiary to Fundamental Rights.

### Phase 2: Attempted Legislative Supremacy
- **25th Constitutional Amendment (1971)**: Introduced **Article 31C**, which declared that laws giving effect to DPSPs under Article 39(b) and 39(c) could not be challenged on the grounds of violating Articles 14, 19, or 31.
- **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)**: Upheld the validity of Article 31C protecting 39(b) and 39(c), but struck down the clause precluding judicial review, firmly establishing the **Basic Structure Doctrine**.

### Phase 3: The Minerva Mills Synthesis
- **Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)**: Parliament had extended Article 31C to cover *all* DPSPs via the 42nd Amendment. The Supreme Court struck down this amendment, stating:

> *"The Indian Constitution is founded on the bedrock of the balance between Part III and Part IV. To give absolute primacy to one over the other is to disturb the harmony of the Constitution. They are like two wheels of a chariot, none less important than the other."* — Justice Y.V. Chandrachud

## Key Principles of Harmonious Construction Today

1. **Complementary and Supplementary**: Fundamental Rights and DPSPs are not antagonistic; they are integrated tools to fulfill the promises of the Preamble.
2. **Expanding Rights via DPSPs**: The Supreme Court has repeatedly read Directive Principles into **Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty)**:
   - **Right to Education (Art. 21A)** inspired by Article 45 (*Mohini Jain, Unni Krishnan* cases).
   - **Right to Clean Environment** rooted in Article 48A (*M.C. Mehta cases*).
   - **Right to Livelihood and Fair Working Conditions** rooted in Articles 39(a) and 42 (*Olga Tellis* case).
3. **Reasonable Restrictions**: Laws enacting DPSPs are considered by courts as strong evidence of "reasonable restrictions" under Article 19(2)–(6) in public interest.

## Summary for Quick UPSC Revision
- Parts III and IV constitute the **"Conscience of the Constitution"** (Granville Austin).
- Neither enjoys unconditional primacy. Harmonious construction preserves individual liberty while facilitating state-led egalitarian welfare reforms.
