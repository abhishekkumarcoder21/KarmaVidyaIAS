## Introduction

Indian agriculture faces an existential climate trilemma: feeding an estimated 1.5 billion people by 2030, coping with escalating climate shocks (*droughts, unseasonal rains, heatwaves*), while mitigating its own ecological footprint (*~14% of national greenhouse gas emissions*). **Climate-Smart Agriculture (CSA)**, formulated by the Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO), provides an integrated strategy across three core pillars: **Sustainably increasing productivity**, **Adapting and building resilience**, and **Reducing/removing emissions**.

## Core Pillars of Climate-Smart Agriculture in India

### 1. Sustainable Productivity & Food Security
- **Climate-Resilient Crop Varieties**: Deployment of bio-fortified, drought-tolerant seeds (*e.g., CR Dhan 801, Pusa Basmati 1847*) that withstand terminal heat and moisture stress.
- **Micro-Irrigation**: Under *Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY)*, drip and sprinkler irrigation enhance water use efficiency up to 90%, preventing soil salinity.

### 2. Adaptation and Climate Resilience
- **Conservation Agriculture**: Zero-tillage farming and crop residue mulching conserve soil organic carbon, preserve micro-fauna, and eliminate crop residue burning.
- **Agroforestry & Agro-biodiversity**: Intercropping with legumes fixes biological nitrogen and shields smallholder farmers from single-crop failures.
- **Weather-Index Crop Insurance**: Integration of satellite monitoring, automated weather stations, and drone surveying under *PM Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)*.

### 3. Mitigating Agricultural Greenhouse Gases
- **Direct Seeded Rice (DSR) & System of Rice Intensification (SRI)**: Drastically curbs anaerobic methane emissions from stagnant paddy water, saving 25–30% irrigation water.
- **Precision Nutrient Management**: Custom soil health cards, neem-coated urea, and nano-urea applications reduce nitrous oxide emissions and ground water leaching.
- **Solarizing Agriculture**: Decentralized solar feeders (*PM-KUSUM*) replace diesel water pumps, providing round-the-clock green irrigation and surplus farmer income.

## Key Challenges to Scalability
- **Smallholder Fragmentation**: ~86% of Indian farmers are small and marginal, lacking capital for mechanized zero-till seed drills and solar pumps.
- **Distorted Subsidies**: Free electricity and subsidized urea disincentivize water conservation and lead to over-application of synthetic fertilizers.
- **Extension Gaps**: Inadequate grassroots training in climate-smart agronomy through Krishi Vigyan Kendras (KVKs).

## Way Forward

A transition to Climate-Smart Agriculture requires reorienting public agricultural subsidies towards eco-schemes (*direct benefit transfers tied to regenerative practices*), strengthening Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs), and deploying precision agri-tech to build an ecologically secure, climatically resilient Indian agricultural economy.
