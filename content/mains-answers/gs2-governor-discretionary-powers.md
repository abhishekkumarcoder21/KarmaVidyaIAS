## Introduction

Dr. B.R. Ambedkar remarked in the Constituent Assembly that the Governor under the Constitution has no functions which he can discharge by himself; his duties are purely advisory. Yet, the friction between gubernatorial actions and elected state cabinets over bill assents (Article 200), appointment of Chief Ministers, and recommending President's rule (Article 356) remains a major stress point in Indian federalism.

## Spheres of Contention

1. **Assent to State Legislation (Article 200)**: Governors sitting indefinitely on bills passed by elected legislatures undermines the principle of parliamentary democracy.
2. **Floor Tests and Government Formation**: Premature or partisan calls for floor tests or discretion during fractured mandates (*e.g., Maharashtra 2019, Arunachal Pradesh 2016*).
3. **Chancellorship of State Universities**: Clashes over vice-chancellor appointments resulting in governance stalemates.

## Constitutional Boundaries and Judicial Precedents

The Supreme Court has consistently circumscribed arbitrary gubernatorial discretion:

| Judicial Precedent | Key Constitutional Doctrine Established |
| --- | --- |
| **Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974)** | Seven-judge bench held that the Governor must act on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers except in extraordinary spheres where discretion is specifically granted by the Constitution. |
| **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)** | Proclamation of Article 356 is subject to judicial review; majority of the ministry must be tested strictly on the floor of the Legislative Assembly. |
| **Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker (2016)** | Discretion under Article 163 is not all-pervasive; Governor cannot summon, prorogue, or dissolve the Assembly without ministerial advice. |
| **State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary (2023)** | Supreme Court ruled that the Governor cannot sit indefinitely on bills under Article 200; withholding assent requires returning the bill with a message "as soon as possible". |

## Recommendations of Expert Committees

- **Sarkaria Commission (1988)**: Governor should be an eminent person outside state politics, selected in consultation with the Chief Minister; discretion under Article 163 should be an exception, not the rule.
- **Punchhi Commission (2010)**: Recommended fixing a 6-month time limit for Governors to decide on bills; proposed state legislative impeachment for Governor's removal.
- **2nd Administrative Reforms Commission (ARC)**: Emphasized that the Governor must act as an institutional umpire rather than an active political player.

## Conclusion

The Governor's office must reflect constitutional morality and neutrality. Preserving healthy cooperative federalism requires adhering to codified timelines, respecting the democratic will of state legislatures, and abiding by the spirit of judicial pronouncements.
