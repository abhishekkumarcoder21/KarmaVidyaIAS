## Introduction

Probity is derived from the Latin *probitas*, meaning honest, upright, and honorable. In public administration, probity goes far beyond passive non-corrupt conduct; it signifies an active, uncompromised fidelity to constitutional values, public interest, and institutional morality.

## Distinguishing Non-Corruption from True Probity

1. **Negative vs. Positive Virtue**: Merely avoiding bribes is compliance with anti-corruption law; probity is proactive empathy, fairness, and optimal stewardship of taxpayer resources (*e.g., ensuring public subsidies reach the last unreached citizen*).
2. **Procedural vs. Substantive Justice**: A bureaucrat might adhere strictly to bureaucratic rules yet remain indifferent to public hardship. Probity demands compassion, moral courage (*parrhesia*), and responsiveness.

## Pillars of Probity in Governance

- **Selflessness and Integrity**: Placing public good above partisan, private, or careerist motives (*Nolan Principles*).
- **Proactive Transparency**: Voluntary disclosure under Section 4 of the RTI Act 2005, rather than defensive concealment.
- **Dedicated Accountability**: Welcoming social audits, citizen charters, and institutional scrutiny.
- **Moral Courage and Intellectual Honesty**: Resisting unlawful orders and tendering fearless advice to political leadership (*Sardar Patel’s vision of the steel frame*).

## Conclusion

As observed by the 2nd ARC, without probity, the best-designed developmental programs fail. Probity is the bedrock of public trust, transforming the administrative machinery into a true instrument of constitutional justice.
